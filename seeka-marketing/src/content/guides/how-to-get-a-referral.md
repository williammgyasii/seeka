A referral moves your application from the pile to a person. Many companies ask employees to recommend people, and some pay them a bonus when a referred hire works out. That means the people inside the company have a reason to help — if you make it easy and safe for them.

You don't need to know anyone yet. You need to find the right person, give them a reason to trust you, and ask for something small and specific.

## Why a stranger would refer you

An employee who refers you is putting a little of their reputation on the line. They will do it when three things are true:

1. **You look like a real fit** for the role, not a mass applicant.
2. **The ask is small.** Forwarding a résumé takes two minutes. Writing a recommendation for someone they've never met is a big ask.
3. **You've done the work.** You've read the job, you know why you fit, and you've said it in two sentences.

Everything below is about making those three things obvious.

## Step 1: Pick the job first, then the people

Referrals work for a specific opening. "Can you refer me to your company?" is hard to say yes to. "Could you refer me for the Operations Analyst role (job ID 4821)?" is easy.

Before you contact anyone, check the role honestly. If you meet most of the core requirements, go ahead. If you're missing several, a referral won't save the application and it costs the referrer goodwill.

## Step 2: Find people who are close to the role

Look for people in this order:

- **Someone on the same team** as the opening, ideally at your level or one above.
- **Someone in a neighbouring team** who works with that team often.
- **Anyone who shares something with you**: the same school, a past employer, the same city, the same professional community.

LinkedIn search works well here: search the company name plus the team or job title, and filter by "Current company". A shared connection or shared background is the strongest opener you have.

## Step 3: Write a short, specific first message

Keep it under 100 words. Say who you are, why you're writing to *them*, and what you want. Don't attach your résumé to the first message — ask first.

> Hi Priya — I'm applying for the Operations Analyst role on your team. I spent three years running reporting for a 40-person logistics team at Kilo, which looks close to what the role describes. Would you be open to a quick question about the team, or to referring me if you think I'm a fit? Happy to send my résumé and a short summary.

Notice what this message does: it names the role, gives one concrete reason you fit, and offers two options. Someone who won't refer a stranger may still answer a question — and that conversation often leads to the referral.

## Step 4: Make the referral effortless

If they say yes, send everything they need in one message:

- The job link and ID.
- Your résumé as a PDF.
- **Three or four sentences they can paste** into the referral form: who you are, what you've done that matches the role, and why you want this job.

The paste-ready summary matters most. Referral forms often ask "How do you know this person?" and "Why are they a good fit?" If you write it for them, the referral happens today instead of next week.

## Step 5: Follow up once, then thank them either way

If you don't hear back, send one follow-up after about a week. Keep it light: "Just bumping this in case it got buried — no worries if now's not a good time."

Whether or not they refer you, thank them. If you get an interview, tell them. People who helped once are often glad to help again, and you've just made a real contact inside a company you care about.

## Common mistakes

- **Asking before reading the job.** If you can't say in one sentence why you fit, you're not ready to ask.
- **Copy-pasting the same message to ten people at one company.** They talk to each other. Pick one or two.
- **Asking for a job instead of a referral.** Employees can rarely hire you. They can forward your application.
- **Overselling.** Claims you can't back up in an interview make the referrer look bad. Stick to what's true.

## The short version

Choose a role you genuinely fit. Find one or two people near that team. Send a short message that names the role and one real reason you fit. Make the referral a two-minute job with a paste-ready summary. Follow up once, and say thank you.
